Accessibility fails silently. We're the alarm.

One in four adults has a disability, yet when a deploy quietly locks them out, nobody on the team feels it — the user just leaves. AccessDrift started as a scanner, until it became clear that everyone audits a snapshot and nobody guards the change between releases. So we built the tool that watches that change, for the small teams who can't afford a specialist.

Mission

What we do

Help small product teams with no accessibility specialist catch the exact moment a release breaks their site — and fix it in plain English — before a disabled user or a legal complaint finds it first.

Vision

Where we're going

A web where accessibility is a continuously-monitored property of every release — tracked like uptime or test coverage — not a one-time audit that decays the moment it passes.

Purpose

Why we exist

So the people who get shut out are never the early-warning system for the people who shut them out. Inclusion shouldn't be a privilege of teams that can afford a specialist.

Trust over reachNever ship a fix we can't stand behind. Low-confidence? Fall back to a human-verified rulebook.
Signal over noiseAlert only when accessibility got worse. A tool that cries wolf gets muted.
Inclusion is the productAccessibility is the whole point — not a compliance checkbox or a badge.
Plain EnglishTranslate "you fail SC 1.4.3" into who it hurts and the exact code change.
Honest heuristicsLabel a guess a guess. We never claim WCAG conformance we can't prove.
Our archetype is the Guardian, with a Sage's honesty. A guardian protects people who can't see the threat themselves — it watches, it warns, it never panics, and it earns trust by being right. That's why we're quiet by default and speak in exactly one signal color: silent until it matters, then unmistakably clear.
